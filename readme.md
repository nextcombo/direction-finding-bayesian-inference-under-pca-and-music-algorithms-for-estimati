# [Direction Finding] Bayesian Inference Under PCA and MUSIC Algorithms for Estimating the Number of Unknown Signal Sources DOA with MATLAB Code

Abstract
Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is a popular method for projecting data onto low-dimensional uncorrelated components, although the optimal number of components is not specified. Similarly, the Multisignal Classification (MUSIC) algorithm is a popular method based on PCA to estimate the direction of arrival (DOA) of sinusoidal sources, but it requires prior knowledge of the number of sources. Therefore, accurately estimating the number of sources is a key issue in the performance of these algorithms. In this paper, we will show that PCA and MUSIC actually return exact joint maximum a posterior (MAP) estimates of uncorrelated steering vectors, although they can only compute this MAP estimate approximately when the correlation is approximated. Then, using Bayesian methods, we calculate for the first time the MAP estimate of the number of sources for both PCA and MUSIC algorithms. Intuitively, this MAP estimate corresponds to the highest probability that the variance of noise still dominates the variance of signals projected onto the signal subspace when there are overlapping multi-audio sources in a linear sensor array. In simulations of overlapping multi-audio sources in a linear sensor array, our precise MAP estimate is far superior to the asymptotic Akaike Information Criterion (AIC), a popular method for estimating the number of components for PCA and MUSIC algorithms.
A. Relevant work
Due to the fact that the MUSIC algorithm is a variant of Principal Component Analysis (PCA), the most common method for estimating K is based on feature-based methods in clustering literature (c.f. [14]), where the most commonly used method is the Information Criterion, such as the Minimization of Description Length (MDL) [15] and the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) for PCA [16]. Despite this, information criteria like MDL and AIC are only asymptotic approximations of maximum likelihood (ML) estimation under infinite data scenarios [15], [17]. Similarly, hard thresholding schemes based on feature values are mostly heuristic [14], and are optimal only in asymptotic settings [18]. Although PCA was invented in the early 20th century [19], [20], it remains one of the most popular data dimensionality reduction methods [21]. In [22], it is shown that PCA is equivalent to the maximum likelihood (ML) estimation of factor analysis models with additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN). Therefore, besides heuristic feature-based methods, non-asymptotic probability methods for estimating K are mostly based on Bayesian theory. However, the posterior probability distribution of the principal vectors and the number of components K in PCA are often complex and do not belong to any known distribution family [23], [24]. Thus, their closed-form representation remains an open problem in the literature [25], [26], [27]. Therefore, the MAP estimation of K can only be calculated through approximate methods in the literature, such as Laplace [28], variational Bayes [23], and sampling-based Markov chain Monte Carlo [24], [26], [27].
According to our understanding, the latest attempt to derive an exact MAP estimate of K in PCA is Theorem 5.1 from [25]. Unfortunately, this theorem imposes a form of standard normal gamma prior on the amplitude and variance of noise, thus still involving two unknown hyperprior parameters for that prior. These two unknown hyperparameters are then estimated before estimating K using heuristic plug-in methods from [25].
B. Paper Contributions
Compared to [25], we will use non-informational priors and impose no extra hyperpriors in this paper. To this end, we derive two new probability distributions, namely the double gamma and the double inverse gamma, in Appendix A. These new distributions will help us calculate the first accurate MAP estimate of K and the posterior mean estimate of signal and noise variances for PCA and MUSIC models without any prior knowledge. Intuitively, our MAP estimate of K is equivalent to selecting the dimensionality of the signal subspace K such that the variance of the signal plus noise exceeds the variance of the projection noise on the highest probability signal subspace.
Our new distributions are essentially the natural form of the joint posterior distribution of signal and noise variances in the PCA model. The motivation behind these new distributions is that under the assumption of Gaussian noise, the variance of the signal plus noise will follow an inverse gamma distribution posterior because the inverse gamma distribution is a conjugate Gaussian distribution. Moreover, since the variance of the signal plus noise must dominate the posterior variance of the noise, the double inverse gamma distribution naturally emerges as the joint distribution under this dominance constraint. Therefore, these new distributions are also useful for general linear AWGN model with unknown source and noise variances as the joint estimation of unknown sources and noise variances.
Due to these new distributions, we will show that, although these methods can only approximate the joint MAP estimate when there is known and unknown noise variances, PCA and MUSIC actually return the uncorrelated main/directed vectors of known and unknown noise variances.
Since the MAP estimate is the best average 0 - 1 loss (also called average L∞-norm), as shown in Appendix B, our MAP estimate of RankK in our simulations is upper-convergent to the AIC method. Additionally, since the precise estimate of K significantly improves the performance of the DFT and MUSIC algorithms, our MAP estimate of K subsequently leads to higher accuracy in DOA and amplitude estimates for both algorithms, especially for overlapping multi-source signals.
In the DOA literature, we recognize that few papers consider multi-source scenarios, even though such cases are frequently encountered in practice. In fact, narrowband and overlapping band sources are examples of this situation. To our knowledge, this is the first study on generalized DOA estimation for multiple sources under Bayesian settings. A simpler version of this paper was recently published in [29], but it merely estimates the binary switching states of multiple sources with known variance of noise.
The organization of this paper is as follows:
1. In Section 2, the linear sensor array is reinterpreted as a linear model in the frequency domain, given the assumption of additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN).
2. In Section 3, the review of PCA, MUSIC, and spectral DTFT algorithms is presented from a new perspective using Hilbert-Schmidt norms' Pythagorean theorem. The complete Bayesian analysis and MAP estimation of these three algorithms are introduced in Section 4. Simulations in Section 5 demonstrate that, especially for overlapping multiple sources in a linear sensor array, an exact MAP estimate outperforms the superior performance of the asymptotic AIC method. Section 6 concludes the document.
